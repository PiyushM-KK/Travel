# Fingerprint registry

Every build gets one row. The gate: a new build must differ from **every** row
below on at least **4 of 6** dimensions. If it fails, change the plan — never
the record. A flattering row is how a registry stops being worth reading.

| Build | Grammar | Nav | Hero | Act shape | Close | Signature move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `skyline-home-sample` | Continuous world | In-world (the itinerary strip is the only progress indicator; no header, no chapter marks) | Full-bleed still, backwater at first light, headline assembling line by line beside an empty ruled column | 9 legs, uneven: 4 short, 1 rest, 1 long peak, 1 rail, 1 reading block, 1 return | Return to start, changed. The opening frame in daylight with the column complete, closing on running text rather than a button | **The itinerary strip.** A column present from the first viewport, empty and ruled. It writes itself one entry at a time as you travel, never erases, and at the end it is the only thing left: the page builds the thing the business sells, in front of you |

## Notes for the next build

Whatever comes next must differ from the row above on 4 of 6. In practice that
means the easy wins are already spent on this side of the registry:

- **Grammar** is the cheapest to change, and the most decisive. Seven of the
  eight are still unused here.
- **The signature move is never a free pass.** A strip that fills in with
  something other than days is the same move with different nouns.
- The **close** used here is "return to start". A second return-to-start build
  would need to differ on all four of the others.
- The **hero** used here is a full-bleed still. A scrubbed clip, type alone, a
  live object or a split stage are all still open — and only the first of those
  needs a real ffmpeg.
