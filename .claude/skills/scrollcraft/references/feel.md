# Feel — the curve, the peak, the check

## The feeling curve

Before a single act exists, write one line per act:

    ACT n — <the emotion> — <the thing on screen that causes it>

Both halves are required. "Act 3 — awe" is not a curve entry, it is a wish.
"Act 3 — awe — the horizon finally tips and you see how far you have come" is.

Then apply the two rules:

1. **No two adjacent acts share a feeling.** If they do, one is filler. Cut it,
   or change what is on screen until it earns a different word.
2. **The curve must go down before it goes up.** A page that is loud from the
   first viewport has nowhere to arrive. Silence in front of the peak *is* the
   peak's asset budget.

Useful words, none of which are "premium": *braced, homesick, unhurried,
implicated, envious, small, trusted, relieved, curious, caught out, at home.*

## The engineered peak

Peak-end rule, applied literally. A reader remembers the most intense moment and
the ending, and almost nothing else. So engineer exactly those two.

**One** peak. It receives:

- the best asset you have, whatever the cost
- the most scroll room — usually 1.5–2× any other act
- the quietest act immediately before it, with no competing motion
- the only moment in the page where more than one device fires at once

A page with three peaks has none. If you cannot decide which act is the peak,
the page does not have one yet, and the honest fix is to cut two candidates.

## The ending

The ending is remembered as vividly as the peak, and it is where the page is
usually thrown away. Endings that work:

- **Return to start**, changed. The reader recognises where they are.
- **A line of running text** rather than a button, when the ask is soft.
- **An object left in a new state** — something the reader altered, still altered.
- **A caption.** Quiet, factual, and it trusts the reader.

Endings that do not: a full-bleed CTA band; a newsletter box; a three-up "next
steps" grid; anything that reads as a footer arriving early.

## The feel check

Run this yourself, on the built page, before you show anyone:

1. **Scroll it once at natural speed, without stopping.** Where did your
   attention actually go? If nothing took it, the peak is not a peak.
2. **Scroll it backwards.** Devices that only work forwards are broken.
3. **Read the whole page with motion disabled.** Does the argument survive as an
   ordinary document? If not, motion is carrying meaning it should not carry.
4. **Look at it on a phone, held one-handed.** Count headline wraps. More than
   three lines in the hero is a failure, not a breakpoint problem.
5. **Name the feeling out loud at each act.** If you say the same word twice in
   a row, you have found your filler.
